RESEARCH

Off-target pharmacology through structure, dynamics, and predictive modeling.

The long-term goal of iMBL is to understand, predict, and ultimately help reduce unintended drug–protein interactions. We combine molecular-level mechanistic studies with data-driven and probabilistic models to investigate drug selectivity, off-target liability, and therapeutic safety.

OUR LONG-TERM QUESTIONS

Questions that guide iMBL

01

Where do drugs go beyond their intended target?

We aim to identify and prioritize plausible unintended drug–protein interactions across proteins and protein families, with particular interest in interactions that may influence safety, efficacy, or patient response.

02

Why do off-target interactions occur?

We investigate the structural, chemical, and dynamic determinants that allow a molecule optimized for one target to recognize another. This includes binding-site similarity, conformational plasticity, molecular recognition, and the energetic basis of selectivity and promiscuity.

03

How confidently can we predict off-target liability?

We develop and evaluate AI/ML and probabilistic models that predict drug–target and off-target interactions while explicitly considering uncertainty, calibration, and applicability domain. A key goal is to distinguish confident predictions from cases where additional data are needed.

04

Can molecular understanding help reduce off-target effects?

By identifying the molecular features associated with selectivity and promiscuity, we aim to support the prioritization and design of compounds with improved target selectivity and reduced off-target liability.

HOW WE APPROACH THESE QUESTIONS

Computational approaches across molecular scales

01

Molecular modeling & structural bioinformatics

Protein structures, binding-site comparison, molecular recognition, ligand–protein interactions, and structure-based hypotheses of selectivity.

02

Molecular dynamics & simulations

Conformational dynamics, interaction stability, mechanistic interpretation, membrane systems, and energetic characterization of molecular interactions.

03

AI/ML, cheminformatics & Bayesian modeling

Molecular representations, drug–target prediction, chemical similarity, uncertainty quantification, probability calibration, and applicability-domain analysis.

RESEARCH FOUNDATIONS

Building on prior work in molecular and integrative biology

The independent direction of iMBL builds on prior research spanning computational drug discovery and molecular simulations, natural-product and protein–ligand interactions, biomolecular structure and dynamics, and data-driven integrative structural modeling. Previous work has included disease-relevant protein systems, membrane and permeability problems, cryo-EM-informed structural analysis, and integration of interaction data such as AP-MS within multiscale modeling frameworks.

These experiences provide the methodological foundation for a more focused long-term program centered on off-target pharmacology, molecular selectivity, predictive uncertainty, and safer therapeutic design.
